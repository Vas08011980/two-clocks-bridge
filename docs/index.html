The Two-Clocks Bridge

Do the two clocks agree? Yearly cohort waves (the slow clock) and daily measurement bursts (the fast clock) are read side by side for ages 18–25, and a preregistered evidence rule says when each kind of evidence can guide decisions.

Vasileios Stavropoulos · Years and Moments project · prototype with simulated data

The evidence rule (preregistered)

Both clocks give a standardised within-person effect. With difference d = yearly − daily:

  1. Undetermined if either 95% CI half-width exceeds .
  2. Agree if the 90% CI of d lies within ± (TOST equivalence).
  3. Diverge if both effects are significant with opposite signs, or d is significant otherwise.
  4. Same direction, different size if both are significant with the same sign and d is significant.
  5. Otherwise undetermined.

Yearly effects from several cohorts are pooled with DerSimonian–Laird random effects; SEs are clustered on persons.

2 · Placement: is the burst sample typical of its generation?

Each burst participant is placed on the cohort distribution of a shared instrument for the same age and the nearest available year. A flat histogram means the sample looks like the cohort; a right-leaning one means more distress.

3 · Same-youth replication outside Australia

Where young people's donated digital histories are linked to their own cohort waves, the person's daily reactivity is tested as a predictor of next-wave wellbeing (adjusting for the prior wave) and compared with the Australian burst estimate.

How to read it

The simulation plants four patterns so every verdict can occur: social media and life satisfaction (same effect on both clocks), gaming and distress (same direction, larger yearly effect), AI companionship and loneliness (opposite signs: short-term relief, longer-term rise), and night-time use and low mood (small effects, too imprecise to judge). The rule recovers these from the noisy estimates without knowing the truth.

Run it yourself: pip install -r requirements.txt && python bridge/bridge.py --seed 2026. Change the seed, the sample sizes in simulate.py, or the margins in bridge.py and the verdicts update.
